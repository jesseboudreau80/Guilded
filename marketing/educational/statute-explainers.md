## FDCPA §809(b) — Debt Validation Rights

**One-liner:**
If a debt collector contacts you, you can demand they prove the debt in writing — and they must stop all collection activity until they do.

**Short version (carousel slide):**
FDCPA §809(b) gives you the right to send a debt collector a written validation request.
Once received, they must:
- Cease all collection activity
- Verify the debt in writing
- Provide original creditor information
If they can't — the account often disappears.

**Full version (post):**
The Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, Section 809(b), is one of the most powerful and underused tools in consumer credit law.

When a debt collector contacts you:
1. You have 30 days from first written contact to send a validation demand
2. Once received, they must STOP all collection activity — calls, letters, reporting
3. They must provide: the original creditor name/address, the amount claimed, and proof of their right to collect
4. If they can't verify: they cannot legally continue collection

Most third-party collectors purchased debts in bulk. Many cannot produce the documentation needed to validate. The account goes silent — and often gets deleted from your credit report as a result.

This right doesn't work retroactively. It requires written notice within 30 days of first contact.

## FCRA §611 — Your Right to Dispute

**One-liner:**
Under federal law, credit bureaus must investigate any item you dispute — and remove it if they can't verify it.

**Short version:**
FCRA §611 gives you the right to dispute any inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable item on your credit report.
Bureaus have 30 days to investigate.
If they can't verify it with the data furnisher — it must be removed.

**What "unverifiable" includes:**
- Wrong balance or account number
- Wrong payment status (open vs. closed, late vs. current)
- Delinquency date that doesn't match the original account
- Account you don't recognize
- Re-aged negative item (extended beyond 7-year window)
- Collection account after statute of limitations

## FCRA §605(a) — The 7-Year Rule

**One-liner:**
Most negative items must be removed from your credit report after 7 years — even if the debt still legally exists.

**Short version:**
FCRA §605(a) limits how long most negative items can appear on your credit report:
- Late payments: 7 years from the date of delinquency
- Collections: 7 years from the original delinquency date (NOT the date sold to collector)
- Charge-offs: 7 years from original delinquency
- Bankruptcies: 7-10 years depending on chapter

Re-aging: When a collector or bureau reports a false (later) delinquency date to extend the 7-year window. This is illegal. Dispute it immediately.

## FCRA §604 — Permissible Purpose for Inquiries

**One-liner:**
A creditor or lender can only pull your credit report if they have a legally valid reason — called "permissible purpose."

**Short version:**
Hard inquiries on your report must have permissible purpose under FCRA §604.
If you see a hard inquiry from a lender you never applied with — that's potentially a violation.
You can send a permissible purpose request demanding they explain why they pulled your credit.
If they can't: the inquiry can be disputed and removed.

## Goodwill Adjustment — Not a Law, a Strategy

**One-liner:**
If you have a strong payment history and a single isolated late payment, a goodwill letter can sometimes get it removed — no legal basis required.

**Short version:**
A goodwill letter is a direct request to the original creditor asking them to remove a late payment as a gesture of goodwill.

When it works:
- You have a strong, long payment history with the lender (5+ years)
- The late payment was isolated — not part of a pattern
- You've paid the account in full
- You explain the circumstances (job loss, medical issue, etc.)

When it doesn't:
- Multiple late payments on the same account
- Account is now in collections
- Creditor has a strict no-removal policy (common with large banks)

This is a request, not a right. Results vary significantly by lender.

## Did You Know — Quick Facts for Social

1. Debt collectors are required by federal law to stop contacting you if you request it in writing. (FDCPA §805(c))

2. You can request a free credit report from each of the three major bureaus — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — once per year at AnnualCreditReport.com. (FCRA §612)

3. Medical debt under $500 was removed from all three major credit bureau reports in 2023.

4. A debt collector cannot legally contact you before 8am or after 9pm in your local time zone. (FDCPA §805(a))

5. Even if a debt is legitimate, if the statute of limitations has passed in your state, a collector cannot sue you to collect — and often cannot legally collect at all.

6. Paying a collection account does not automatically improve your credit score. Ask about "pay for delete" or validate first to see if the account can be disputed instead.

7. You have the right to add a 100-word "statement of dispute" to your credit report if a dispute is not resolved in your favor. (FCRA §611(b))
