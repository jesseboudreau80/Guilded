## Carousel 02: "The 3 types of things on your credit report that can be challenged"

**Format:** Educational breakdown
**Best for:** Mid-campaign, builds credibility

Slide 1 — HOOK
> "Not everything negative on your credit report deserves to be there."
> "Here are the 3 categories worth challenging — and how."

Slide 2 — CATEGORY 1: ERRORS
> "Inaccurate information"
> Wrong balance, wrong date, wrong status, wrong creditor name
> Law: FCRA §611 — bureaus must investigate and remove if unverifiable
> "30 days. That's the window."

Slide 3 — CATEGORY 2: VIOLATIONS
> "FDCPA violations — collector misconduct"
> Debt sold without documentation · no validation provided · contacting after cease request
> Law: FDCPA §809(b) — demand validation. They stop or they comply.
> "Many collectors can't produce the paperwork."

Slide 4 — CATEGORY 3: OUTDATED
> "Time-expired items"
> Most negatives must be removed after 7 years (FCRA §605(a))
> Re-aging is illegal — check the original delinquency date
> "The 7-year clock starts when you first went delinquent — not when the debt was sold."

Slide 5 — SUMMARY
> [Three columns: Errors · Violations · Outdated]
> "Each requires a different strategy. Each has a different legal basis."
> "Guilded identifies all three across your full report."

Slide 6 — CTA
> "See what's on your report — and what you can do about it."

## Carousel 03: "FDCPA vs FCRA — which law applies to your situation"

**Format:** Decision-tree / comparison
**Best for:** Educational series, high engagement potential

Slide 1 — HOOK
> "Two federal laws protect you from credit abuse."
> "Most people use the wrong one — or use both wrong."

Slide 2 — FCRA OVERVIEW
> "FCRA — Fair Credit Reporting Act"
> Controls what appears on your credit report
> Who can see it · How long it stays · How it must be reported
> "Dispute inaccuracies directly with the bureaus"

Slide 3 — FDCPA OVERVIEW
> "FDCPA — Fair Debt Collection Practices Act"
> Controls how DEBT COLLECTORS behave
> Original creditors are NOT covered — only third-party collectors
> "Demand validation · Cease contact · Sue for violations"

Slide 4 — THE DISTINCTION THAT MATTERS
> "The same account can involve BOTH laws"
> "Original creditor reports inaccurately → FCRA §611 dispute to bureaus"
> "Debt collector contacts you → FDCPA §809(b) validation demand"
> "Same debt. Different strategies. Different timing."

Slide 5 — WHO IS A DEBT COLLECTOR?
> "Debt collector = anyone collecting a debt they didn't originate"
> Portfolio Recovery Associates ✓
> Midland Funding ✓
> LVNV Funding ✓
> Capital One (original lender) ✗ — FCRA only

Slide 6 — CTA
> "Guilded identifies which law applies to each account on your specific report."

## Carousel 04: "Why most dispute letters fail (and what works instead)"

**Format:** Common mistake → correct approach
**Best for:** High engagement, shares well in the group

Slide 1 — HOOK
> "I've seen thousands of dispute letters in this community."
> "Here's why most of them don't work."

Slide 2 — MISTAKE 1
> "Generic templates"
> "The bureaus see thousands of identical letters. They know it's a template."
> Correct: Letters that cite the specific account, specific date, specific inaccuracy
> "Specificity is credibility."

Slide 3 — MISTAKE 2
> "Disputing everything at once"
> "Scatter-shot disputes signal you don't know what you're doing"
> Correct: Prioritize by legal strength — FDCPA violations before FCRA disputes
> "The sequence is the strategy."

Slide 4 — MISTAKE 3
> "Wrong law for the account"
> "FDCPA only covers debt collectors — not original creditors"
> "FCRA applies to bureau reporting — not collector contact"
> Correct: Match the statute to the situation

Slide 5 — MISTAKE 4
> "No follow-up system"
> "Bureaus have 30 days. Most people forget to follow up."
> Correct: Track your disputes, escalate if they go silent, use the 100-word statement right
