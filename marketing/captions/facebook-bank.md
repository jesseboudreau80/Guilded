## CATEGORY: FDCPA / FCRA Education

**Caption C — FDCPA §809(b) explained**
> If a debt collector contacts you about a debt — even one you think you might owe — you have a right that most people never use.
>
> FDCPA §809(b): send them a written validation request. They must stop ALL collection activity until they provide:
> · The name and address of the original creditor
> · The amount of the debt
> · Proof of their right to collect
>
> Most collectors can't produce all three. The account often goes silent — or disappears from your report.
>
> This isn't a trick. It's a federal law. And it has a 30-day window from first contact.
>
> (Educational content. Not legal advice.)

---

**Caption D — FCRA §611 explained**
> If something on your credit report is wrong — or can't be verified — you have the right to dispute it.
>
> FCRA §611 requires credit bureaus to investigate every dispute within 30 days. If they can't verify the item with the data furnisher, they must remove it.
>
> "Can't verify" covers more than you think:
> · Wrong balance
> · Wrong payment status
> · Closed account showing open
> · Re-aged delinquency date
> · Account that doesn't belong to you
>
> The bureaus don't remove items voluntarily. But they can't keep items they can't verify.
>
> (Educational content. Not legal advice.)

**Caption F — "Did you know?" education series**
> Most people don't know there are two completely different federal laws protecting them from credit reporting abuse.
>
> FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act) covers what can be on your report, how long, and under what conditions.
>
> FDCPA (Fair Debt Collection Practices Act) covers how collectors can contact you, what they must prove, and what they're prohibited from doing.
>
> Using the wrong law for your situation dramatically reduces your results. The strategy depends on who you're dealing with and at what stage.
>
> Guilded identifies which law applies to each account in your report. [link]
