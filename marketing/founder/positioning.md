# Founder Positioning Guide — Jesse Boudreau

## The Core Narrative

Jesse ran DIY Credit Repair 101 for years before building Guilded.
That community context is the credibility. The tool is the output of years of watching what doesn't work.

**The honest version of the origin story:**
"I've been in this space a long time. I've watched the cycle repeat:
someone asks for help → gets hit with 50 DMs from credit repair salespeople →
pays $200/month for a template → the template doesn't fit their situation →
nothing changes → they leave, embarrassed.

I got tired of watching that happen. I built the thing I wish had existed.
Not a credit repair company. Not a coach. A tool — built on consumer law,
built on the same knowledge we talk about in this group every day."

---

## What to be public about

- The community backstory (25,000 members, years of watching this problem)
- The educational philosophy (teach the law, not the shortcut)
- What the tool cannot do (score guarantees, legal representation)
- How data is handled (PDF deleted after processing, PII masked)
- The stack and approach (AI reads your report, consumer law is the framework)
- Pricing (transparent, no hidden upgrade walls)

## What to avoid

- Specific user success stories without explicit, documented permission
- Income claims or projections
- "My results" stories that could be misread as a guarantee
- Attacking specific competitors or credit repair companies by name
- Implying the tool replaces legal counsel

---

## Tone calibration by platform

**Facebook group post (DIY Credit Repair 101):**
Conversational. First-person. Vulnerable about the problem. No product hype.
Start with the human observation, not the product.

**Direct message (beta invite):**
Personal. Short. Frame them as collaborators, not customers.
"I'd love your feedback from someone who actually knows what a real credit report looks like."

**Product copy (website, preview page):**
Precise. Transparent. Specific about what it does and doesn't do.
Every claim supported by a visible mechanism (e.g., "FCRA §611" not just "dispute items").

---

## Phrases that work in this community

- "Built for the DIY crowd — not the people who want to sell you a program"
- "The law is already on your side. The tool just shows you where."
- "Every letter is built around your specific report — not a template"
- "Free to start. See what's actually on your report before you decide anything."
- "I'm still in the group. Still answering questions. This is my community too."

## Phrases to avoid

- "Skyrocket your score"
- "Guaranteed results"
- "Watch your credit score improve in 30 days"
- "I deleted $X in debt" (even if true — too easily misread as a guarantee)
- "Limited spots" or countdown timers
- "This is the only tool that..."
- Anything that sounds like a sales funnel

## Sample DM templates

**Beta invite (warm):**
> "Hey [name] — I've seen you asking sharp questions in the group for a while.
> I built something that analyzes credit reports with AI and identifies FCRA/FDCPA opportunities specific to your situation — not a generic template.
> Free to start. I'd genuinely love your feedback from someone who knows what they're looking at.
> Want early access? No obligation."

**Beta invite (cold-ish):**
> "I've been building a tool for people like us — the ones who'd rather know the law than pay someone to maybe know it.
> It reads your credit report and shows you exactly what's disputable and why, with the letter framework to back it up.
> It's free to start. If you ever want to give it a try and tell me what I got wrong — I'd really value that."

## Community engagement rules

1. Always answer questions in comments publicly when possible — visibility builds trust
2. When sharing Guilded content: educational first, product second (or not at all)
3. Never pitch when someone is venting or sharing a bad experience
4. Give freely in the group — the credibility compounds
5. Acknowledge when Guilded can't help: "Guilded isn't designed for X. For that, I'd look at..."
